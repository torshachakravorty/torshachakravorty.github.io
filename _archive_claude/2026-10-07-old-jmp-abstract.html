Building State Capacity in Stages: Brokers in public service delivery

  • Draft available on request

    • Bureaucratic processes are often completed in sequential stages whose workers are not interchangeable. Thus, the ability of states to deliver services depends on capacity at each stage of the production process. This paper tests for spillovers when the state expands capacity in only one of multiple complementary production stages. We exploit plausibly exogenous district-level variation in government staffing for routine public service applications in a large Indian state. Contrary to general intuition, citizen use of informal brokers increased in districts receiving more new workers per capita. To complement the administrative data, we collect original survey data on broker fees to quantify the de facto costs citizens face. Because informal broker roles lack standard definition, we benchmark our empirical findings against multiple theoretical models of broker competition and citizen choice. We document new broker entry, show that broker value-added persists across non-expanded production stages, and demonstrate which market structures best account for the observed price and application dynamics. Our findings illustrate the importance of understanding the organization of informal markets in the process of building state capacity.
