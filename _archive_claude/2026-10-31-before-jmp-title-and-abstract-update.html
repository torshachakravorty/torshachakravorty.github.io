Building State Capacity in Stages: Brokers in Public Service Delivery

Citizens need to establish eligibility for public benefits, and many pay private agents (brokers) to help obtain them. We study how this broker market responds when the government makes it easier to apply directly. A staffing reform in a large Indian state added workers who help submit applications, while leaving approving officials unchanged. Using pre-existing differences in district exposure to the reform, we find that a 1 percent staffing increase raises broker-mediated applications by 1.7 percent, compared to 1.1 percent for direct applications, and induces broker entry. Yet direct applications become faster, reducing brokers’ average speed advantage. These patterns rule out a model where public workers purely substitute for brokers and, under maintained assumptions, one where brokers only obtain priority from approvers. A model where public workers make brokers more productive can explain joint results. Our findings highlight the role of informal markets in building state capacity.
